About Me

I’m Shawn McNurney, an engineering professional and current undergraduate student pursuing a B.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Oregon State University. My interest in data science actually started in a warehouse. While working as a Slot Info Lead, I developed data-driven slotting logic to organize over 30,000 unique inventory items. Seeing how a solid dataset could completely transform physical operations was a lightbulb moment for me.

Today, I work as a Facilities Engineer specializing in building management systems (BMS) and controls for a healthcare campus. Every day, I work with massive amounts of data generated by HVAC, refrigeration, and electrical systems. I’m constantly analyzing steam generation metrics or programming microcontroller sensor arrays, but I realize there is a distinct gap between collecting this data and actually using it to predict problems. I'm building my data science skill set so I can bridge that gap, combining my hands-on physical engineering background with advanced analytics to build smarter control systems.

Career Goals

Working in facility engineering and industrial utility infrastructure, I see firsthand how much operational data gets left on the table. Modern building management systems (BMS) generate continuous logs from HVAC, electrical, and refrigeration units, yet most of it is still used reactively—only after equipment has failed or drifted out of spec.

My primary career goal is to move into a hybrid role, such as a Systems Integration Engineer or Industrial Data Analyst, where I can apply data science directly to industrial automation and controls. Instead of waiting for a pump or chiller to fail, I want to build predictive maintenance models that detect early signs of equipment degradation and flag issues before they cause downtime. I also want to develop systems that dynamically optimize energy consumption using historical facility data.

Looking back at the job postings we analyzed earlier in this course, employers in this space aren't just looking for someone who knows a long list of trendy tools—they want people who can anchor core data skills to real operational problems. That aligns directly with my background. Starting with the data-driven slotting logic I built as a Slot Info Lead for over 30,000 inventory items, and now working daily with steam generation metrics, BMS controls, and ESP32 sensor arrays, I already understand the physical systems generating the data.

To bridge the gap between collecting data and acting on it, I am focused on strengthening my Python, C/C++, and data analysis skills to match my hands-on hardware experience. Ultimately, my goal is to build practical, data-driven systems that keep critical physical infrastructure running reliably and efficiently.

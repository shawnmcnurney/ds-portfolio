About Me

I’m Shawn McNurney, an engineering professional and current undergraduate student pursuing a B.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Oregon State University. My interest in data science actually started in a warehouse. While working as a Slot Info Lead, I developed data-driven slotting logic to organize over 30,000 unique inventory items. Seeing how a solid dataset could completely transform physical operations was a lightbulb moment for me.

Today, I work as a Facilities Engineer specializing in building management systems (BMS) and controls for a healthcare campus. Every day, I work with massive amounts of data generated by HVAC, refrigeration, and electrical systems. I’m constantly analyzing steam generation metrics or programming microcontroller sensor arrays, but I realize there is a distinct gap between collecting this data and actually using it to predict problems. I'm building my data science skill set so I can bridge that gap, combining my hands-on physical engineering background with advanced analytics to build smarter control systems.

Data Science Career Goals Reflection

Working in facility engineering and industrial utility infrastructure, I see firsthand just how much data gets left on the table. Modern building management systems generate endless logs from HVAC, electrical, and refrigeration units, but most of it is only used reactively—after something has already broken or drifted out of spec.

My main career goal is to move into a hybrid role, like a Systems Integration Engineer or Industrial Data Analyst, where I can apply data science directly to industrial automation. Instead of just fixing equipment when a pump fails, I want to develop predictive models that flag equipment degradation before a failure happens. I also want to build systems that optimize energy consumption dynamically based on historical facility data. Reaching this goal means strengthening my software and data analytics skills to match my hands-on hardware experience. Ultimately, I want to use data science to build actionable systems that keep our physical infrastructure running smoothly and efficiently.
